Transparent scores

No black-box numbers

Every score is computed from public store data with a formula you can read — and check in the source.

Popularity

0–100

How much demand a keyword shows, judged by the apps that rank for it.

  • 40Rating volume of ranking apps (log scale)
  • 22Breadth of results
  • 20Average star rating
  • 18Share of apps running ads

Difficulty

0–100

How hard it is to break into the results for this keyword.

  • 40Share of “authority” apps (≥ 50K ratings)
  • 25Share of apps running ads
  • 20Breadth of results
  • 15Share of apps rated ≥ 4★

Opportunity

0–100

High demand you can realistically compete for. Sort by this first.

opportunity = popularity
  × (100 − difficulty) / 100
  • 56popularity 80, difficulty 30 — go for it
  • 18popularity 20, difficulty 10 — small niche
  • 8popularity 80, difficulty 90 — crowded

These are free, public-data estimates — not Apple Search Ads’ first-party popularity. Use them to compare keywords with each other. Full methodology →

How it works

Up and running in 3 steps

From install to your first insight in about a minute.

  1. 1

    Add your apps

    Click + (or press A) and search by name, package (com.spotify.music) or App Store id. Apps on both stores merge into one entry.

  2. 2

    Analyze keywords

    Type a keyword and press Enter. You get scores for it and 5 similar keywords, plus your app’s position on each store.

  3. 3

    Read & export reviews

    Switch to Reviews, filter by words and stars, compare apps, then press Ctrl/⌘+E to export.

Private by design

Your research stays on your machine

No cloud, no sign-up, no OwlASO server in the middle. The app talks straight to the public store endpoints.

  • No account, no telemetry

    Tracked apps, folders and keyword history are stored on your device. There’s no analytics and no OwlASO server.

  • Local-only API

    Web mode binds to 127.0.0.1 and rejects foreign Host headers and cross-site requests (DNS-rebinding & CSRF protection).

  • Hardened desktop app

    Sandboxed renderer with context isolation, a strict CSP, http(s)-only external links, and every permission request denied.

  • Safe exports

    Review text that starts with =, +, - or @ is neutralised, so a review can’t run as a spreadsheet formula.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Short answers here — the docs go deeper.

Is OwlASO really free?

Yes. It’s open source under the MIT license — no paid tier, no seats, no sign-up.

Where does the data come from?

App Store: Apple’s public iTunes Search API and customer-review feed. Google Play: there is no official public reviews API, so OwlASO uses the open-source google-play-scraper, with an HTML fallback.

Requests go straight from your computer to the stores. Results are cached for 5–15 minutes to keep traffic low.

How accurate are popularity and difficulty?

They’re estimates built from public signals (rating volume, star ratings, ads, result breadth), not Apple Search Ads’ first-party data. They work best for comparing keywords against each other. See the formulas.

Why do I only get about 500 App Store reviews?

Apple’s public review feed stops at 10 pages of 50 reviews per storefront (country). Pick All countries to read every storefront at once. Google Play allows far more: up to 5,000 per fetch, and Full data reads 1,000 per language — Go deeper raises that to 5,000.

Is there a Mac or Linux version?

OwlASO runs on macOS and Linux from source today (Node.js 22.12+). The prebuilt installer is Windows-only for now; npm run dist:mac and npm run dist:linux build your own DMG or AppImage. Install guide.

Does rank history update by itself?

History is recorded whenever you analyze a keyword — one snapshot per day per keyword, store, country and language. Re-analyze your keywords on different days and the trend arrows and charts fill in.

Will the stores block me?

Normal use is fine. OwlASO caches results, merges duplicate requests and backs off when a store says “slow down” (it honours Retry-After). Hammering the stores with huge batches can still get you throttled for a while.
